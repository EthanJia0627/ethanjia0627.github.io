I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at University College London. I study how humanoid robots learn as one task flows into the next, without a reset or a clear boundary between tasks.

Past experience rarely fits a new situation exactly. How can a robot decide what to reuse, act to find out what it doesn't yet know, and try a new approach? What it learns from that attempt should help shape its next decision.

SPIRE: When does an old motor skill help?

Yiyang Jia, Mingyang Lyu, Gongce Cao, et al.

Can a controller recognise a change of situation and reuse parts of an earlier skill without being told which task it is facing? SPIRE tests this by selectively rewinding what the controller learned before.
